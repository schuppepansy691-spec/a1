1. The 1928 Genesis: Irving Schott and the First Motorcycle Jacket

In 1928, Russian immigrant patternmaker Irving Schott designed a specialized leather jacket commissioned by a Long Island motorcycle distributor. Named the "Perfecto" after his favorite cigar, it was the first garment in history to utilize an industrial zipper fastener rather than traditional horn buttons.

Prior to 1928, motorcyclists wore button-up aviation coats or wool overcoats that flapped violently in the wind and allowed freezing drafts to penetrate the buttonholes. The Double Rider revolutionized protective outerwear overnight.

2. The Biomechanics of the Asymmetrical Front Zipper

The most defining architectural feature of the Double Rider is its asymmetrical diagonal zipper, running from the left hip up toward the right shoulder. When a rider leans forward over low motorcycle handlebars, a centered vertical zipper bunches outward like a rigid tent pole against the chest.

The diagonal zipper allows the jacket front to bend naturally along the riderâ€™s torso without bunching, providing a streamlined, ergonomic riding fit.

3. Overlapping Wind Flaps and Gale-Force Draft Elimination

When the asymmetrical zipper is fully pulled up to the throat, the two front leather panels overlap completely across the riderâ€™s chest. This creates a double-layered leather barrier that blocks 100% of piercing winds at 80 mph, shielding the riderâ€™s vital organs from hypothermia.

4. Bi-Swing Action Backs and Forward Arm Reach Kinematics

Because heavyweight 3.5 oz horsehide does not stretch, a standard flat-back jacket restricts forward arm movement. The Double Rider incorporates an articulated "bi-swing" (or action-back) pleat behind each shoulder seam, backed by internal elastic webbing.

When reaching forward for handlebars or steering wheels, the pleats open up smoothly, providing full arm extension without pulling the hem upward at the waist.

5. Kidney Support Belts and Drop-Tail Lower Spine Protection

Vintage motorcycle riders endured punishing vibrations over unpaved roads. The integrated leather waist belt with heavy nickel buckle cinched the lower hem tightly, providing lumbar kidney support while preventing wind from blowing up the back of the jacket.

6. Tactical Pockets: The Aviator Map D-Pocket and Snap Coin Pocket

Early Double Riders featured the iconic curved "D-pocket" on the left chest, designed so a rider could reach inside with his right hand while operating the throttle with his left. The small snap-flap coin pocket at the right waist held toll coins and house keys securely.

7. Heavy Metallurgy: #10 Talon and Italian Raccagni Hardware

To withstand decades of abrasive stress, JacketDistrict outfits its Double Riders with solid brass #10 gauge Italian Raccagni Super R zippers, featuring individually machined, hand-buffed teeth that glide smoothly without jamming or tearing leather teeth tapes.

8. Lapel Snap Architecture: Converting High Collar to Wide Rever

When riding in milder weather, the upper collar unzips and folds outward into wide, notched lapels anchored by solid brass dome snaps, preventing the collar points from fluttering against the rider's helmet.

10. The Cultural Icon: From Brando to the Modern Gentleman

Immortalized by Marlon Brando in *The Wild One* (1953) and adopted by generations of rock icons, the Double Rider has transcended counterculture rebellion to become an enduring cornerstone of modern masculine tailoring.

11. Custom Sleeve Pitch Calibration for Riding Stance

In our Mercer Street atelier, each bespoke Double Rider is tailored with custom sleeve pitch: sleeves are set slightly forward at the armhole, eliminating underarm tension when gripping handlebars or vehicle steering wheels.

12. The Timeless Triumph of Asymmetrical Armor

By combining functional industrial engineering with full-grain Horween horsehide, the JacketDistrict Double Rider represents the ultimate synthesis of protective armor and timeless sartorial power.

13. Gusseted Sleeve Cuffs and Gauntlet Glove Integration

The sleeves of the Double Rider terminate with heavy brass zippers backed by interior leather gussets. When unzipped, the cuff expands to accommodate heavy winter gauntlet gloves; when zipped down tight, it forms an airtight seal around the wrist, preventing wind from blowing up the riderâ€™s arms.

14. Curved Kidney Panels and Drop-Tail Lower Hem

When seated on a motorcycle in an aggressive forward lean, standard jacket backs ride up, exposing the lower spine to freezing wind and road debris. The Double Rider features an elongated, curved drop-tail kidney panel that stays planted securely over the beltline, providing continuous lower back warmth and lumbar protection.

15. The Eternal Crown of Protective Outerwear Design

From mid-century speedways to modern urban streets, the Double Rider remains the ultimate expression of purposeful engineering, masculine authority, and indestructible leathercraft.

Every element of the Double Rider—from its overlapping asymmetrical wind flaps to its articulated bi-swing shoulder pleats and heavy Raccagni brass zippers—was designed for high-speed functional performance. When tailored in heavyweight front-quarter Horween horsehide, it becomes an indestructible armor that commands respect and provides lifetime windproof protection on and off the road.

The Double Rider represents the ultimate marriage of form and function, celebrating nearly a century of protective motorcycle heritage and timeless sartorial power across generations of passionate riders.
